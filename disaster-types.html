Emergency Action Protocols

Expert-approved safety steps to take before, during, and after major disaster situations.

Flood Protocol

Flooding is the most common natural hazard worldwide. Flash floods can occur within minutes of heavy rain, dam failures, or storm surges, carrying debris and washing out structures.

Before the Flood

  • Identify local elevation levels and determine safe uphill evacuation routes.

  • Seal birth records, deeds, and critical papers in waterproof bags.

During the Flood

  • Do not walk or drive through flowing water. Just 6 inches can sweep you off your feet.

  • If trapped inside a building, move to high floors, avoiding enclosed attics.

After the Flood

  • Avoid contact with standing floodwaters; they may contain electrical lines or chemicals.

Flood Preparation

Ensure you have sandbags, utility gloves, rubber boots, and portable pump equipment ready.

Build Flood Kit Now

Cyclone & Hurricane Protocol

Cyclones bring destructive winds exceeding 120 km/h, catastrophic storm surges, and coastal flooding. Preparing property structural joints is critical.

Before the Cyclone

  • Fit storm shutters or cover window panes with thick marine plywood boards.

During the Cyclone

  • Shelter in solid interior corridors, small central rooms, or bathrooms.

After the Cyclone

  • Do not inspect outdoor damage until warning systems declare it safe.

Cyclone Preparation

Gather emergency plywood, heavy duty rope, water bottles, and hand-crank warning radios.

Build Cyclone Kit

Earthquake Response Protocol

Earthquakes occur abruptly without warnings, leading to structural failures and dangerous falling debris. Knowing Drop, Cover, and Hold On can save your life.

Before the Earthquake

  • Secure heavy wall cabinets, bookcases, and heaters to studs using steel straps.

During the Earthquake

  • Drop, Cover, and Hold On. Move under heavy desks and hold onto the legs.

After the Earthquake

  • Inspect gas and electricity lines. Turn off main valves if damage is suspected.

Earthquake Preparation

Stock helmet safety gear, solid work boots, flashlights, water bottles, and emergency gas wrenches.

Build Earthquake Kit

Fire Response Protocol

House fires and regional wildfires spread with incredible speed. Pre-mapping exit doors and keeping smoke alarms active is vital.

Before the Fire

  • Install smoke detectors inside every room and test batteries monthly.

During the Fire

  • Crawling low below soot clouds helps avoid inhaling toxic smoke gases.

After the Fire

  • Do not enter burnt structures until fire marshals declare them secure.

Fire Preparation

Acquire fire extinguishers, fire blankets, respirator masks, and emergency lights.

Build Fire Kit

Severe Storm Protocol

Supercell thunder, high lightning strikes, and gale wind hazards can occur rapidly, knocking out grid power lines and phone networks.

Before the Storm

  • Unplug sensitive home electronics to protect against lightning surges.

During the Storm

  • Avoid concrete walls and floors, which can conduct lightning currents.

After the Storm

  • Avoid fallen power lines and stay clear of large puddles nearby.

Storm Preparation

Acquire power banks, emergency flashlights, hand-crank radios, and solar lanterns.

Build Storm Kit

Extreme Heatwave Protocol

Heatwaves are prolonged periods of hot weather that strain cardiovascular health. Proper hydration and cooling strategies save lives.

Before the Heatwave

  • Insulate air conditioning ductwork and seal drafty window gaps.

During the Heatwave

  • Drink plenty of water and electrolytes, avoiding sugary or caffeinated drinks.

After the Heatwave

  • Continue monitoring signs of heat exhaustion: nausea, dizziness, or headaches.

Heatwave Preparation

Stock electrolyte packs, cooling towels, water containers, and emergency spray bottles.

Build Heatwave Kit

Landslide & Mudslide Protocol

Landslides occur when soil layers slide down slope routes. Heavy rain, earthquakes, or wildfires can trigger landslides suddenly.

Before the Landslide

  • Monitor soil drainage paths on hillsides and watch for tilting fence posts.

During the Landslide

  • Evacuate landslide paths immediately. Mudflows travel faster than people can run.

After the Landslide

  • Stay clear of mudflow areas; slide slopes can collapse again without warning.

Landslide Preparation

Prepare safety helmets, solid work boots, flashlights, water bottles, and emergency tools.

Build Landslide Kit

Extreme Weather Protocol

Severe cold weather, blizzards, and winter ice storms bring sub-zero hazards. Protecting pipes from freezing and preventing hypothermia are priorities.

Before Extreme Cold

  • Add insulation wrap to water pipes exposed to cold drafts.

During Extreme Cold

  • Dress in warm layers, covering exposed skin to prevent frostbite.

After Extreme Cold

  • Gradually warm frozen pipes to prevent structural damage or cracks.

Extreme Cold Preparation

Stock thermal blankets, wool clothing, matches, fuel supplies, and backup food boxes.

Build Winter Kit

Rescue Logistics

Active Search and Rescue Coordination

In the direct aftermath of heavy earthquakes, windstorms, or severe coastal flooding, local emergency dispatchers execute critical search and rescue sweeps. SafeAlert coordinates communication logs and location nodes to expedite these responder sweeps.

By registering your household safety blueprints and out-of-town checkpoints, you make it possible for municipal warden crews to verify family headcounts and locate survival cells quickly.

Municipal Alert Colors

Learn how meteorological watch groups define danger thresholds for public advisories.

Green (Advisory)

Minor weather alerts. Normal operations continue. Check checklist supplies.

Yellow (Watch)

Weather hazard is possible. Assemble emergency kit dry packs.

Orange (Warning)

Hazard is imminent. Board up glass windows and prepare generators.

Red (Emergency)

Immediate severe danger. Secure group members and evacuate immediately.
